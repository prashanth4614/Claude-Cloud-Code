**Verdict: revise before G1 approval.** Strong visual mechanism; current wording overstates physics and makes the forward run sound like a recommended technique.

**Physics**

- **Backward:** if the planted foot grips, the body tends to topple backward **relative to the person**, still along the train’s direction. Facing alone doesn’t guarantee a fall. Drop “head first” and “no step to save it”; neither follows inevitably.
- **Sideways:** a sideways stumble is plausible; spinning isn’t inevitable. Rotation needs an off-centre force, such as one shoe catching. Better: “Your body keeps moving along the platform, throwing you off balance.”
- **Forward:** successive recovery steps *can* prevent a topple and allow gradual slowing. Running itself doesn’t necessarily slow you. Replace “Face forward and run it out” with: “Several steps can spread the slowdown—but they cannot guarantee your balance.”
- **Foot/ghost:** “Your planted foot slows sharply; your upper body keeps moving” beats “your feet stop.” Keep the ghost’s motion relative to the **platform**; inertia isn’t a force pushing Max.
- **Forces:** arithmetic works: 70 kg at 10 km/h, stopped in 0.1 s, gives ≈1,940 N or **2.8 body weights**; over 1 s, ≈194 N or **0.28**. These are average **horizontal braking forces**, excluding vertical landing loads and peaks. Neither stopping time is established by “one step.” “Less force on each step” also overclaims. Keep numbers out; say longer slowing means lower average braking force for the same momentum change. [Impulse reference](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/8-2-impulse).

**Facts**

10 km/h works as an **illustrative speed**, not a documented platform standard or safety threshold. Show the dial reaching **10** before contact; currently it ends at 15. “More than twice a typical comfortable walking speed” is defensible; “your walking speed” universalizes it. [Walking study](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2024.1357172/full).

The deaths line needs tightening. 6,760 over January 2014–May 2025 averages roughly **590/year**, not 500; an average doesn’t establish “every year.” These are all train falls, with **platform-gap deaths separately categorized**. Placing that tally over the gap misleadingly associates categories. Prefer description-only context, or: “Mumbai recorded 570 deaths from moving-train falls in 2024”—explicitly not step-off deaths. The Print and The Week repeat one PTI report, not independent corroboration. [PTI categories](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/06/10/bom29-mh-train-deaths.html), [2024 tally](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/922-commuter-deaths-in-mumbai-locals-in-just-5-months-including-210-fell-from-running-trains).
